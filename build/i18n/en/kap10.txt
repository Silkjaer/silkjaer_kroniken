Three years earlier · Harboøre, November 1893
@53285a962fd6
The sentence about the sea was not Nedergaard’s invention. Fifty kilometres to the north, twenty-six fishermen from Harboøre had drowned in November 1893, and at the joint funeral the Inner Mission pastor <t1>Carl Moe</t1> interpreted the disaster as a call to those left behind. The address set off a nationwide controversy over the Inner Mission’s view of Christianity and made an impression all along the west coast.
@147d7cd0b25c
But the distance to Harboøre was also a distance in experience. <t1>Sigurd Enggaard Poulsen</t1>, who grew up by the fjord as a grandchild of the revival’s people, weighed the two against each other: “It was more ‘the other pastor’, the sea, that had spoken than it was the pastor at Harboøre.” The phrases from Harboøre recurred, and the songs with them. But the disasters were the dunes’ own.
@24f31b18fd5d
On 20 May 1894 a sea-boat was wrecked on the inner bar off Søndervig; five men drowned, two were saved. The same day <t1>Trækfuglen</t1> (the Migrant Bird) from Nymindegab was lost a couple of nautical miles out with all her crew, four men. On 1 April 1896 came <t2>Forsøget</t2>. Thirteen men in two years — “everyone knew everyone on the dunes, and in one way or another almost everyone was related to one or more of them.”

Getsemane
@8c9fa8f6325c
The revival began with meetings in private homes. So many came that the parlours could no longer hold them, and three and a half years after the wreck the mission house <t1>Getsemane</t1> (Gethsemane) stood finished at Haurvig. The master mason Kristen Jensen had built it, and on 23 October 1899 it was consecrated by the Bishop of Ribe, <t2>Carl Viggo Gøtzsche</t2>. It became the centre for meetings, mission weeks, youth work and Sunday school on the southern dunes.
@d062b036e4a4
That a bishop came and consecrated the house says something about what it was. Getsemane was not a breakaway congregation but an Inner Mission meeting place with the church’s sanction — a house beside Haurvig Church, not instead of it. The church had baptism, communion and burial; the mission house had the weekdays, when lay people could themselves speak, pray and sing several times a week.
@e10bfd6c4ce3
The name was hardly chosen by chance. Gethsemane is the place of prayer and of deep distress — the garden where the cup did not pass. The same year Thabor was built inland on Holmsland, and in 1910 <t1>Ebenezer</t1> followed at Sønder Lyngvig. The movement spread along the dunes.
