It was not a muddle in the papers but a custom: the new wife gave her daughter her predecessor’s name — in the house where the predecessor had lived and died. And the family had done it before. When Niels’ uncle Esper lost his first wife Johanne Cathrine in 1856, his first daughter with his new wife was named Johanne Cathrine the year after. The same course, the same choice, a generation apart — and it was the uncle who did it first. [S69] It is above all her children who carry the name out into the country.
