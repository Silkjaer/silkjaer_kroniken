<t1>S118</t1> <t2>Vridsløselille as an institution in 1913: the house of correction on Vridsløselille Mark (from 1933 Vridsløselille State Prison) was organised on the Philadelphia system — sentences served in solitary confinement day and night, a mask outside the cell, a ban on conversation and stalls in church and school; compulsory masks were only made voluntary in 1924, and the principle of isolation remained central until the Penal Code of 1930, in force in 1933. The Penal Code of 1866 at the same time laid down a reduction of the term for sentences served in solitary confinement — isolation was in itself considered an aggravating element — so that the actual time served by prisoners in the house of correction lay between 6 months and 3½ years, against sentences of 8 months to 6 years; the reduction was maintained until 1933. § 13 of the Act set the reduction at one third for the first three years; eighteen months of hard labour in the house of correction thereby became twelve, which corresponds exactly to the prison register’s date of release (27 April 1914, one year after the start of the sentence). The conversion has been checked against the text of the law; that the sentence was in fact served in solitary confinement is the law’s main rule and an inference from the date, not information in the prison register.
